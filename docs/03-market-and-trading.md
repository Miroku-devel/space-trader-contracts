# 3. Market & Trading

Trading is the heart of the game. You buy goods in one system and sell them in another for a profit. This chapter explains the market, the ten tradeable goods, how prices are formed, and the danger of illegal cargo.

---

## The ten goods

There are ten tradeable commodities. Each occupies one cargo bay per unit. Their **base prices** (before any adjustments) are listed below — these are the reference numbers; actual prices swing widely based on the system.

| Good | Base Price | Illegal? | Notes |
|------|-----------|----------|-------|
| **Water** | 30 | No | Cheap staple; valuable in deserts and during drought. |
| **Furs** | 250 | No | Valuable in cold or lifeless worlds. |
| **Food** | 100 | No | Valuable during crop failure. |
| **Ore** | 350 | No | Bulk metal; valuable during war. |
| **Games** | 250 | No | Luxury; valuable in bored, artistic societies. |
| **Firearms** | 1,250 | **Yes** | Illegal in many systems; high profit, high risk. |
| **Medicine** | 650 | No | Valuable during plague. |
| **Machines** | 900 | No | Industrial goods; valuable where workers are scarce. |
| **Narcotics** | 3,500 | **Yes** | Very lucrative but illegal; huge risk. |
| **Robots** | 5,000 | No | Top-tier goods; valuable where labor is short. |

**Illegal goods** (Firearms and Narcotics) cannot be sold openly everywhere. If the local government forbids them and the police inspect you, you can be fined, have cargo confiscated, or worse. See chapter 9.

---

## How a price is built

A good's price in a given system is computed from several layers stacked on top of each other. You do not need to calculate anything — the market shows you the final number — but knowing the layers helps you predict where to buy and sell:

1. **Base + Tech Level** — the foundation. Each good's price rises or falls a fixed amount per tech level. High-tech goods (Robots, Machines, Medicine) get *cheaper* as tech rises; raw goods (Water, Food, Ore) get *more expensive* as tech rises.
2. **Government desire** — if a government particularly wants a good, its price is multiplied by about 1.33 (a third higher).
3. **System size** — bigger systems are a little cheaper.
4. **Resource** — a "cheap" resource cuts the price by ~25%; an "expensive" resource raises it by ~33%.
5. **Status event** — an active event (drought, war, plague…) raises the price by ~50%.
6. **Random offset** — each system has a small permanent price quirk, so no two systems are identical.

### Buy vs. sell price

- The **sell price** is the market price above.
- The **buy price** you pay is slightly higher — a markup that shrinks as your **Trader** skill rises. A skilled trader buys closer to the true market price and keeps more profit.
- If your **police record** has dropped to "Dubious" or worse, every sale pays about **10% less** (the black market takes its cut). Keep your record clean to trade at full value.

---

## Trading in practice

1. Open **Trade** while docked at a system.
2. The list shows every good, its price, how much is available, and whether it is illegal here.
3. Enter a quantity and buy what you can carry and afford.
4. Fly to another system and open **Trade** again to sell.

A good rule of thumb: never sell for less than you paid unless you must. The profit is in the difference between systems.

---

## The Search screen (your price radar)

The **Search** button opens a scanner that compares prices across nearby systems for you. Instead of flying blind, you can see at a glance:

- Which neighboring systems sell a good **cheapest**.
- Which neighboring systems buy a good **dearest**.

Use Search before every long haul. It turns guesswork into a plan and is one of the most powerful tools a new player has.

---

## Supply and demand over time

Systems slowly replenish the goods they produce and consume the goods they demand. A system you stripped of cheap Water today will have more tomorrow. Prices also drift as status events begin and end. Nothing is permanent, which keeps the galaxy fresh.

---

## A note on cargo space

Every good takes exactly one cargo bay per unit. Your ship's cargo capacity is limited (the Flea holds only 10 units). Upgrading to a bigger ship, or buying the **5 extra cargo bays** gadget, directly increases how much you can haul — and therefore your profit per trip. See chapter 5.

# 7. Encounters & Combat

Space is not empty. Every journey can bring you face to face with another ship. This chapter explains who you might meet, what choices you get, and how combat works.

---

## Random encounters

While traveling, you may be intercepted. The type and frequency of encounters depend heavily on the **government** of the space you are in (chapter 2): lawless systems breed pirates, orderly ones are patrolled by police, and busy ones are full of traders.

You might meet:

- **Pirates** — hostile ships that want your cargo and your credits. They come in many sizes, from weak Gnats to deadly Wasps and special raider ships (Dragonfly, Mantis, Scarab, Scorp).
- **Police** — lawful ships that may simply pass by, inspect you, or — if you have a bad record — attack.
- **Traders** — seemingly friendly merchant ships. Beware: some "traders" are pirates in disguise, and even honest ones can turn on you.

The bigger and more valuable your ship looks, the more interesting the encounters become.

---

## Your choices during an encounter

When an encounter begins, you are usually given a set of actions (not all appear in every situation):

| Action | What it does |
|--------|--------------|
| **Attack** | Open fire and start combat. |
| **Flee** | Try to escape. Success depends on your Pilot skill and relative speeds. Failing to flee may let the enemy get free hits. |
| **Surrender** | Give up to a pirate/trader; you may lose cargo or credits but keep your life. |
| **Submit** | Comply with a police inspection. |
| **Bribe** | Offer money to make trouble go away (works better in corrupt systems; risky with honest police). |

Choosing wisely is often more important than fighting. A small trader with no weapons is usually better off fleeing or bribing than trading shots with a pirate.

---

## How combat works

Combat is real-time but you can **pause** to think. Key ideas:

- **Shields first, hull second.** Enemy fire depletes your shields; only when shields are gone does it damage your hull. Keep your shields up and your hull intact.
- **Targeting components.** You can aim at specific parts of the enemy ship — its weapons, shields, engines, or power core. Knocking out the enemy's shields or weapons makes the fight much easier. Likewise, the enemy tries to disable *your* systems.
- **Your weapons and Fighter skill** determine how fast you win. More and stronger weapons, plus a higher Fighter skill, mean quicker kills.
- **Escape is always an option** (unless something pins you). If a fight goes badly, flee.

When you destroy an enemy, you may loot its cargo and credits, and your **reputation** as a fighter grows. When an enemy destroys you, see chapter 10.

---

## Police encounters in detail

Police behave based on your **police record** (chapter 9):

- If your record is clean, police usually just pass by or perform a routine inspection.
- If you are carrying **illegal goods** and they inspect you, you face fines, confiscation, and a worse record.
- If your record is already poor, police may attack on sight.
- You can try to **bribe** police, but in strict systems (Military, Fascist) this can backfire and make things worse. In corrupt systems (Anarchy) bribes are cheap and effective.

Attacking or killing police is one of the fastest ways to ruin your record — think carefully before you fire on the law.

---

## Pirates and "traders"

Some encounters labeled as traders are actually pirates. If you attack a trader who was harmless, you may gain a bad reputation and police attention. If a trader attacks you first, defending yourself is fair game. Reading the situation — and your record — matters.

---

## Survival tips

- Fly with shields and at least one weapon once you can afford them.
- Improve **Pilot** to escape bad fights and **Fighter** to win good ones.
- Keep an **Escape Pod** (chapter 5) so a lost fight is not the end.
- When outmatched, flee early rather than gamble on a lucky shot.

# 2. The Galaxy

The universe of Space Trader — Contracts is a procedurally generated galaxy of **800 star systems**. Every game is different. Each system is its own little world with its own economy, government, and mood.

Understanding the galaxy is the key to making money. Prices, dangers, and opportunities all depend on where you are.

---

## Star systems at a glance

Every star has four important properties you can see on the map and in its info panel:

1. **Tech Level** (how advanced the civilization is, from 0 to 7).
2. **Political System** (what kind of government rules it, one of 16 types).
3. **Resource** (a special trait that makes some goods cheap or expensive).
4. **Status** (a temporary event affecting the local economy).

There is also a **size** (Tiny, Small, Medium, Large, Huge) that slightly affects prices, and some stars are special **Wormholes** or **Habitable Moons** (see chapter 6).

---

## Tech Levels

Tech Level (abbreviated TL) runs from 0 (stone age) to 7 (utopian future). It drives almost everything about a system's market.

| TL | Name |
|----|------|
| 0 | Pre-Agricultural |
| 1 | Agricultural |
| 2 | Medieval |
| 3 | Renaissance |
| 4 | Early Industrial |
| 5 | Industrial |
| 6 | Post-Industrial |
| 7 | Hi-Tech |

**Why it matters:** Each good has a base price that goes *up or down* depending on the local tech level. Primitive worlds pay a lot for advanced goods (machines, robots, medicine) and sell raw materials (water, food, ore) cheaply. Advanced worlds do the opposite. The sweet spot for a trader is to buy on one end of the tech spectrum and sell on the other.

Some goods only appear in systems within a certain tech range, so a very low-tech or very high-tech world may simply not trade in certain items.

---

## Political Systems

There are 16 types of government. Each one shapes the local economy and the kind of trouble you will find there:

Anarchy, Capitalist, Communist, Confederacy, Corporate, Cybernetic, Democracy, Dictatorship, Fascist, Feudal, Military, Monarchy, Pacifist, Socialist, Technocracy, Theocracy.

Different governments have different **minimum and maximum tech levels** and different **trade rules**. For example:

- **Anarchy** and **Feudal** worlds tend to be low-tech and lawless, with many pirates and little police presence. They are the easiest places to trade in illegal goods.
- **Military** and **Fascist** systems are high-tech and heavily policed. They are also the only places where you can buy a **Police** cruiser (and only if your record is good).
- **Pacifist** worlds are low-tech and peaceful, with few pirates.

Each government also sets how often **pirates, police, and traders** show up in its space, how willing the locals are to accept a bribe, and how they react to illegal cargo. You will learn these the hard way or by paying attention to the map.

---

## Resources (why some goods are cheap or expensive here)

Each system may have a **resource** — a geographic or economic trait — that changes prices for specific goods. There are many, for example: Mineral Rich, Mineral Poor, Desert, Sweetwater Oceans, Rich Fauna, Lifeless, Weird Mushrooms, Special Herbs, Artistic, Warlike, Rich Soil, Poor Soil.

- If a system's resource makes a good **cheap**, that good costs about **25% less** there.
- If a system's resource makes a good **expensive**, that good costs about **33% more** there.

This is one of the biggest levers in the game. A "Sweetwater Oceans" world sells Water for next to nothing; a "Desert" world pays a premium for it. Learn to match resources to goods and you will never run out of profit.

---

## Status (temporary events)

From time to time a system suffers an event that shakes up its market. Events happen randomly and can also clear on their own. Common events include:

- **At War** — Ore and Firearms become expensive.
- **Plague** — Medicine becomes expensive.
- **Drought** — Water becomes expensive.
- **Boredom** — Games and Narcotics become expensive.
- **Cold Spell** — Furs become expensive.
- **Crop Failure** — Food becomes expensive.
- **Lack of Workers** — Machines and Robots become expensive.

When an event hits, the affected goods cost about **50% more** than usual. A system "suffering from drought" is a screaming buy-low/sell-high opportunity for anyone carrying Water.

---

## Size

Systems come in five sizes. Larger systems have slightly **lower** prices (more competition and supply), while tiny outposts are a bit more expensive. The effect is modest but worth noting when you compare two nearby systems.

---

## Putting it together

A profitable trade route is born when you find a system that is **cheap** in one good (low tech, or a "cheap" resource, or simply a low price) and another system that is **expensive** in that same good (high tech, an "expensive" resource, or an active event). The wider the gap, the fatter the profit.

The **Search** screen (chapter 3) does much of this comparison for you automatically.

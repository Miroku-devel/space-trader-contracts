# 10. Survival, Strategy & Score

This chapter covers the things that end a run — and how to avoid them — plus how to "win" a game that has no forced ending, and how your final score is calculated.

---

## Staying alive

The single biggest threat is losing your ship. Two things protect you:

1. **An Escape Pod** (bought at the shipyard). If your hull hits zero and you have a pod, you are ejected and survive.
2. **Insurance** (bought at the bank, requires the pod). If your insured ship is destroyed, the bank pays you its full value.

### What happens when your ship is destroyed

- **With an Escape Pod:** you are picked up by the Space Corps, dropped at a nearby port, and given a replacement **Flea** (the smallest ship) plus 500 credits (added to debt if you cannot cover it). Your cargo is lost, your crew scatters, and your active missions are cleared. If you had insurance, you also receive a payout equal to the destroyed ship's value. Painful, but you live to trade again.
- **Without an Escape Pod:** this is **GAME OVER**. Your run ends and a final score is shown.

So the Escape Pod is not optional for serious play — it is the difference between a setback and the end.

---

## Common ways players get into trouble

- Flying a fragile ship (Flea) into pirate-heavy space with no weapons.
- Carrying illegal goods through strict, policed systems.
- Letting a loan balloon to the debt ceiling and then being unable to leave a system.
- Ignoring hull damage until a single hit destroys the ship.
- Attacking police and spiraling into a "Psycho" record.

All of these are avoidable with a little caution.

---

## General strategy

- **Early game:** trade legal goods along safe, short routes. Build credits. Buy cargo bays and a better ship.
- **Mid game:** add shields and one weapon. Take ride/deliver missions to pad income. Start manhunts once armed.
- **Late game:** fly a tough ship (Wasp, Police cruiser, Termite), take hero missions, and push reputation toward Elite. Keep insurance active.
- **Always:** watch fuel, keep an escape pod, and manage your police record.

---

## Winning the game: retirement

There is no "final boss." The game is a sandbox, and you "win" by choosing to **retire**. From the **Dashboard**, you can retire your commander at any time. Retirement ends the run and shows your final score — the game's way of saying "you made it."

Retiring is appropriate when you feel your fortune is enough and you want a clean, triumphant ending rather than risking everything in one more fight.

---

## Your final score

When you retire (or are killed), the game computes a **score** based mainly on two things:

- Your **net worth** at the end (cash + ship + cargo − debt). Very large fortunes are "compressed" so the score stays readable, but wealth is always the dominant factor.
- The **number of warps** (travels) you made. Finishing rich *and* efficiently — in fewer warps — boosts the score.

The result is shown as a percentage-style rating. A high score means you built a great fortune, ideally without wandering the galaxy forever. Your end screen also summarizes your time played, warps, kills, police record, reputation, cash, debt, net worth, missions completed, and escapes — a nice snapshot of your career.

---

## One last tip

The galaxy rewards patience and information. Use **Search** to find the best prices, use **Routes** to plan efficient loops, and never fly farther than your fuel and your nerve allow. Fly safe, Commander.
